# Flak Battery — architecture notes

The detailed design notes for `games/flak-battery/`, moved out of the root [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) so they load when you work in this directory rather than on every session (Claude Code picks up a `CLAUDE.md` in the directory it is working in). The root file keeps the rules that break things; this file has the reasoning and the model. Read it before changing the engine or the shell here. Past decisions are in [docs/DECISIONS.md](../../docs/DECISIONS.md).

- **The shop opens with three branches, not nine** (`BRANCH_UNLOCK`/`openBranches`/`branchUnlocked`). Nine tracks at once, at 1 mount and 0 scrap, was the onboarding wall — and the three cheapest rows (Cooling/Breech/Interlock at 13) all moved the same heat gauge, so the most tempting first buy was the least visible one. **The gate is on `experience(w)` — the best wave ever reached, carried in `research.best` — not on the current wave**, so a veteran restarting has the whole tree in the first shop and never re-earns it. `canAfford`/`buyUpgrade` both refuse a locked branch, so the shell cannot sell one. Engine tests that are about what a branch *does* call the `veteran(w)` helper rather than depending on the opening being wide.
- **The thermal three are one card in the shop, and three branches in the engine.** Grouping is presentational only: `stats(w, gun)` folds over `BRANCHES` and a test pins one-stat-per-branch, so merging Cooling/Breech/Interlock for real would cost structure to fix a presentation problem.
- **There is no Damage branch** — damage is derived from Calibre and Velocity through `roundDamage(shotR, shotSpeed)`, and the shop shows **DPS** (reload and barrels folded in, heat deliberately not). A row that says "more damage" beats every conditional row, so it was the first purchase every run; making it a *result* is what puts the tree's real choices first. Removing or repricing either input must keep a maxed gun within a few percent of ×3.1, which a test pins.
- **Upgrades are seven single-stat branches** (`UPGRADES`), not the four bundles they were until v30 (convergence left for research in v36). `stats(w, gun)` folds over `BRANCHES` rather than listing them, so a branch added and not spread would otherwise resolve to `undefined` everywhere. When adding one, keep the invariant a test enforces: **each branch moves exactly one stat** (two documented exceptions: Munitions' `bounces` alongside `pierce`, and Calibre/Velocity each moving the derived `dmg`), and splitting a branch must *divide* its cost rather than reprice its children upward.
- **The lead marker reports one intercept per distinct round speed** (`aimGroups`/`predictHits`), because a mixed battery has no single lead. Per-gun speed must include `gunStats().spd` — reading `stats()` alone collapses every gun back into one group, which is the bug it was built to fix. Marched by distance, not time, so several speeds cost one walk.
- **Deep waves arrive with an escort** (`SWARM_WAVE` 30 onward, `SWARM_FULL` thirty waves later): small cubes flying a lemniscate over the leading column, intercepting rounds that meet them (`makeSwarm`/`stepSwarm`/`swarmPos`, collision at the top of the chain loop in `stepShots`). Two dials that move **one after the other, never together** — density to `SWARM_FULL`, then health — because density is answered by volume of fire and health by damage per round, and escalating both at once makes the two halves of the shop indistinguishable as answers. The escort pays score but no scrap, cannot breach, cannot hold a wave open, and is anchored to the leading chain so it dies with the column. Nothing random: wave N flies the same pattern every run.
- **Portrait mounts are per gun type** (`towed` / `deck` / `bunker` / `mound`), and so is the elevation offset (`PORTRAIT_RISE`). What a gun stands on is read before its barrel — it is the widest thing on the card — so outriggers, a bolted deck plate, an armoured casemate and a ball behind a low earth lip are four different weapons at a glance. The v49 art was **designed on a canvas and ported coordinate for coordinate**: each gun is one entry in `PORTRAIT_ART`, written in the small `portraitKit` vocabulary, back to front. The ion's near lip is drawn *last*, so the ball sits behind it rather than on the ground. Every gun pivots on a sphere (defenders are spheres). In the portrait the second barrel is drawn *above* the main one, although the engine offset is positive — below, it runs through the cannon's recoil cylinder; the board draws the real offsets.
- **`gunStats(w, type)` is the only place a gun type's numbers come from** — never `GUN_TYPES` directly. Marks (permanent per-type research) multiply through it. In the *shell*, the same rule applies to what the shop prints: `statDisplay(gun, key, upgrades)` is the one place, feeding both the Stats block and the branch previews. They were computed separately until v44 and diverged the moment a gun type or a mark was researched.
- **Research is a ladder, a rung every ten waves** (`RESEARCH_UNLOCK`, gated on `experience(w)` like the branch ramp): nothing before wave 10, then standard-cannon marks, Autocannon, Convergence, Railgun, branch depth, Mortar, Ion. Every `research*` verb refuses a locked category, so the shell cannot sell one. The research screen is ordered by that table, and locked rungs are drawn, not hidden.
- **[briefing.html](briefing.html)** is a reference page (guns, power-ups) linked from the pause menu. **Every number on it is read from the engine at load** — that is the point of it. Gun one-liners live on `GUN_TYPES[t].blurb` so the shop and the briefing quote one sentence; the tactical prose ("how to play this") is the page's own.
- **Convergence is research, not a branch, and it contracts rather than engages.** It was a per-emplacement scrap branch until v36, which never made sense — the guns share one focal point, so the engine read the *best* convergence on the battery and every other mount's purchase did nothing. It is now one permanent RP track (`CONV_MAX`/`convergeLevel`/`researchConverge`) applying to the whole battery, including mounts built later. The focal point sits at `focusDefault(L)` — **five rungs of the serpentine**, derived from `L.ROW_GAP` so it is five rungs on either board rather than a pixel count that means different things — and **contracts inward** to follow the leading command ship as it closes, down to the floor the researched level allows (`focusFloor`). It never travels outward: far out the fan is an asset, since several mounts on one distant point sweep a dense column. `aimPointFor` returns `range` alongside `x`/`y`. Don't reintroduce a *degree*-of-convergence interpolation — DECISIONS 2026-08-08 records why partial convergence is worse than either end.
- **Convergence is also what makes a decapitation possible** (8.2s with it against 57.5s without, at wave 18). Anything that changes it should re-check that, or the command ship quietly becomes unkillable again.
- **Perspective is draw-only.** `perspScale(y)` shrinks things standing on the ground plane (craft, road, tether) toward the top of the board; shots and the battery deliberately opt out, and `seg.r` is never touched. The road is a single filled ribbon because stroking it either steps at the corners or beads where translucent caps overlap.
- **[engine.js](engine.js)** is the entire simulation: geometry, enemy chains, guns/battery, upgrades, pickups, and the `step(w, dt, firing)` function that advances the world by one frame. It has **no DOM, no canvas, no timers, no randomness beyond a seeded LCG** (`rollDrop`) — this is what makes it fully unit-testable. Never add `document`/`canvas`/`Date.now()`/`Math.random()` calls here; keep those in the HTML shell.
- **[flak-battery.html](flak-battery.html)** is the playable shell: it `import`s `engine.js` as a module, owns all rendering (canvas draw calls), input (pointer/keyboard/touch aiming), the shop UI, and the `requestAnimationFrame` loop. Game *rules* belong in `engine.js`; anything about pixels, DOM, or timing belongs here.
- **No standalone build.** Flak Battery used to ship a generated single-file `flak-battery-standalone.html` (plus a `build.mjs` to regenerate it), an early distribution experiment from before the other three games existed. It was retired in v26: it had to be regenerated after every change to `engine.js`, the shell, or anything in `shared/`, and a stale one meant the render test was silently checking old code — a standing trap for no benefit, since nothing distributed the file. All four games now boot their real shell through **[tools/inline.mjs](../../tools/inline.mjs)** in memory instead (see the render-test notes in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)).
- **[engine.test.js](engine.test.js)** is organized by subsystem (path geometry → chains/segments → shielding/deflection → recoil → overdrive/heat → aiming curves → upgrades → battery/guns → pickups → splitting → breach/wave/run lifecycle → full-run simulations). When adding engine behavior, find the matching section rather than appending to the end.
- Core simulation model, useful when tracing a bug:
  - `world` (built by `createWorld`) holds `chains` (arrays of segments moving along a precomputed serpentine `path`), `battery` (multiple `guns`, shared aim/streak/overdrive), `shots`, `pickups`, `bits`/`floaters` (pure visual junk).
  - Enemies are **chains of segments** referenced by arc-length `s` along the path (see `atS`/`segPos`); killing a middle segment produces `recoil` (chain gets pushed back) instead of the chain instantly shortening.
  - `KIND` defines enemy archetypes (armored, volatile, shielded, regen, splitter, carrier, head) with per-kind special-cased behavior inside `damageSeg`/`stepChains`.
  - Overdrive/heat is per-gun (`heat`, `cool`, `locked`) and streak/tier is shared on the battery. **The upgrade branches (`BRANCHES`, all seven) are per-emplacement too**: they live on the gun, and `stats(w, gun)` resolves them — read from that, never the raw `UPGRADES` tables, and never without saying which gun. `buyUpgrade`/`canAfford`/`upgradeCost` all take a mount index. A new mount arrives with an empty tree however deep the others are, which is the intended balance: a fifth gun competes against deepening the four you have, and the prices were left alone precisely so the five-fold bill bites.
  - `setGunType(w, mount, type)` charges a **retrofit** in scrap (`retrofitCost`, derived from the type's `refitBase`), on top of having researched the type at all; pass `free: true` only when building a mount rather than converting one.
  - **Two currencies, kept apart.** Scrap is the run — kills in, tiers/barrels/mounts/retrofits out, gone when the run ends. **Research points** are what a whole run was worth (`researchEarned`, paid once by `awardResearch` at game over, from the wave reached) and they persist. RP buys gun types permanently (`researchGun`) and the last two tiers of each branch (`researchDepth`; tiers 1..`FREE_TIER` are free to everyone, and `tierCap(w, branch)` is what `upgradeCost`/`canAfford` enforce). `w.gunUnlocks` is a *projection* of `research.guns`, re-derived by `syncGunUnlocks` — it is deliberately **not** in the snapshot, or a save would smuggle a gun past current research and pin a resumed run to what was known at save time. As with Choke Point's armory the engine touches no storage: the shell loads and saves it, and `resetRun` leaves it alone.
  - **Barrels are per emplacement** (`gun.barrels`, `barrelsOf`, `barrelCost(w, mount)`, `buyBarrel(w, mount)`) — they were battery-wide until v28. `hydrate` reads the old top-level `snap.barrels` as a fallback onto every mount.
  - **Power-ups are prised open, not picked up.** A shot claiming one mid-air has always been the main route; it now takes `PICKUP_HITS` rounds, and every one of them is consumed. `PICKUP_R` is what a crate is *drawn* at and `PICKUP_CATCH_R` is what it is *caught* at — deliberately separate, because drawing one bigger to make it visible must not also make it easier to earn. Letting one fall into `CATCH_BAND` still claims it outright: that route costs position instead of ammunition.
  - Shots carry `arc` and `travelled`: a lobbed (mortar) round cannot hit anything inside `MORTAR_ARM`, which is how "reaches over the front rank" is implemented on a board with no third axis. `BARREL_OFFSETS` are **lateral** (perpendicular to the aim), so extra barrels fire parallel rather than in a fan; flanking rounds are scaled by `SUB_BARREL_DMG`/`SUB_BARREL_R`. Both the mount art and the shop portrait read those offsets from the engine so the drawing cannot drift from the geometry.
  - A breach opens the shop (`shopOpen` + `retry`) instead of respawning the wave; `nextWave` reads `retry` to know it is a retry rather than an advance. **A wave is always one chain** — splitting it across columns was tried and rejected, and the note above `hpScale` explains why (each chain grows its own armored head, so two columns of 39 carry ~21% more health than one of 78).
  - One `step(w, dt, firing)` call per frame drives cannon → pickups → chains → shots → breach/wave-clear checks, in that order; hit-stop short-circuits everything else at the top of `step`.
